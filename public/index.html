MAZAR-E-QUAID ARABIAN SEA KARACHI HERITAGE
National Monument

The Mazar is Karachi's quiet landmark.

Mazar-e-Quaid stands in the heart of the metropolis, its white marble geometry creating a calm visual counterpoint to the movement of the city around it.

  • Completed in 1971: Designed by architect Yahya Merchant, the monumental mausoleum serves as the final resting place of Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah.
  • Pure White Marble: Crafted from brilliant white marble arched over a 54-square-meter platform, the structure reflects coastal sunlight throughout the day.
Clifton Heritage

Mohatta Palace

An ornate 1927 summer residence built with pink Jodhpur and yellow Gizri stone, Mohatta Palace stands as a sanctuary of art, culture, and architecture in seaside Clifton.

Location: Clifton, Karachi Style: Indo-Saracenic Revival
Colonial Heritage

Frere Hall

Completed in 1865 in Venetian Gothic style and set within the sprawling Bagh-e-Jinnah gardens, Frere Hall houses the iconic ceiling mural painted by celebrated artist Sadequain.

Location: Saddar Civil Lines Style: Venetian-Gothic

Empress Market

Archival Briefing

Karachi Heritage Notes

Mazar-e-Quaid

Constructed between 1960 and 1971 in white marble from a design by Yahya Merchant. The sanctuary houses the tomb of Pakistan's founder and serves as the architectural heart of the city.

Mohatta Palace

Commissioned in 1927 by Shivratan Chandraratan Mohatta as a summer residence in Clifton. Designed by Agha Ahmed Hussain in Indo-Saracenic revivalist style using Gizri and Jodhpur stone.

Frere Hall

Inaugurated in 1865 in honor of Sir Bartle Frere. Designed by Henry Saint Clair Wilkins in Venetian Gothic style, featuring local yellow Gizri stone and Sadequain's legendary mural Arz-o-Samawat (Heaven and Earth).

Empress Market

Opened in 1889 during Queen Victoria's Golden Jubilee era. Designed by James Strachan with a prominent 140-foot clock tower, anchoring the Saddar trade district.
